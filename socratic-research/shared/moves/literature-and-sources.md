# Literature, sources and contribution

Use this move to examine how the work relates to what has already been written: where it sits in a debate or field, whether its claimed gap or novelty holds, whether it represents its sources faithfully, and how its reading of a primary text stands up.

Every rule in `evidence-integrity.md` applies with particular force here. Rely only on sources the researcher has supplied or that have been verified in this session. Mark everything else `[SOURCE NEEDED]`.

## Use when

- The contribution claim depends on what others have or have not done.
- The work claims a gap, a first, or novelty.
- A draft cites sources, and the citations carry weight.
- The work interprets a primary text, record or document.
- The researcher needs a search plan.

## 1. Positioning

- Which debate, field or line of work does this enter?
- What are the main positions in it, and what is each trying to explain or achieve?
- Where does the researcher's claim agree with each, and where does it disagree?
- Who would be the most informed critic of this work, and what would they say is already known?

## 2. Kind of contribution

- Does the work address an omission, answer a recognised objection, resolve an ambiguity, test an untested assumption, introduce a method, apply an existing idea to a new domain, synthesise separate literatures, provide new evidence, or differ only in vocabulary?
- Original compared with what? What is the comparison class?

## 3. Gap claims

- What search supports the claim that the gap exists? Which databases, terms and dates?
- Did it cover synonyms, neighbouring fields that use other vocabulary, other languages, and non-journal sources?
- Is the gap one of absence (nothing exists), insufficiency (what exists is weak), fragmentation (pieces exist separately), disagreement (the literature conflicts), or lack of testing (claims exist but are untested)?
- Is it a gap in publications, in knowledge, or in practice?
- Can this project close the gap, or only part of it?
- Why would filling it matter?

## 4. Novelty

- Is the originality in the topic, the question, a distinction, an argument, a synthesis, a method, an application, the evidence, or the result?
- Is it substantive or terminological?
- Does the same result follow from an existing framework?
- Does the value of the contribution depend on a result not yet obtained?

## 5. Source fidelity

Claim by claim, for each sentence that depends on a source:

- Which supplied passage supports this sentence?
- Does the sentence keep the source's scope, modality and qualifications?
- Does it turn possibility into actuality, correlation into causation, an example into a general result, a criticism into a consensus, or one author's view into the field's?
- Is it a quotation, a paraphrase, or the researcher's own inference presented as the source's?

If the passage is not supplied, say so and mark the sentence `[SOURCE NEEDED]`. Do not fill the gap from general knowledge.

## 6. Interpreting a primary text

- What does the passage explicitly state, what does it imply, what does it presuppose, and what is left ambiguous?
- What is its role in the wider work? Does the reading fit that role?
- Develop at least two plausible readings. For each: the textual evidence for it, its advantages, its difficulties, and its assumptions.
- Is any claim being attributed to the author that the text does not support?
- What would settle the choice between readings: another passage, the author's other works, historical context, the original language?

## 7. Search plan

When literature is missing:

- Which distinct claims in the work need support?
- For each: the relevant field or subfield; search terms, synonyms and neighbouring concepts; the kinds of source needed (primary text, review, empirical study, formal result, critique, replication); what a source would need to show to be relevant.
- Include searches for critical and opposing work, not only supporting work.
- Follow citation chains backwards (what the key sources cite) and forwards (what cites them).

Distinguish known references from suggested leads. Never present a lead as an established citation.

## Prior-work labels

Report the result of any check with one of these labels and nothing stronger:

- "close prior work found" (with references or links);
- "related work found; the gap is [X]" (with references or links);
- "no prior work found in [N] searches" (with the queries);
- "not checked".

## Artefacts

- **Positioning statement**: the debate, the precise unresolved problem, the intervention, how it differs from the nearest alternatives, and the limits of the search behind it.
- **Contribution claim**: one sentence, with a more cautious fallback, and the conditions under which the originality claim should be withdrawn.
- **Fidelity lists**: claims adequately supported; claims needing weaker wording; claims needing a source or removal.
- **Search plan**: claims, terms, source types, and both supporting and critical searches.
