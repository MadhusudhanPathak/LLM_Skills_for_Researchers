# Operationalisation, measurement and validity

Use this move when a concept must become something observable or measurable, when a number stands in for something that matters, or when the question is whether a measure, test or evaluation captures what it claims to. It is the bridge between conceptual or theoretical work and empirical or technical work.

## Use when

- A concept or property is about to be measured.
- A metric, score, benchmark, scale, indicator or coding scheme is used as evidence.
- A proxy stands in for a harder-to-observe target.
- The validity of a measure or evaluation is in question.

## 1. Before the measure

- What theoretical work does the concept do, before we talk about how to measure it?
- Which dimensions of it can be observed directly, which only inferred, and which not at all?
- Is the target property behavioural, internal, dispositional, relational, counterfactual, or normative?
- What would someone who cared about the concept say a measure must not leave out?

## 2. Competing operationalisations

- Give two competing ways to operationalise the concept. Find a case on which they disagree. Which one does your argument need?
- Could something score well on the measure while lacking the property? Could it have the property and score badly?
- Does the measure depend on something other than the property, such as general ability, difficulty, familiarity, or the procedure itself?
- What does the measure leave out, and does the argument need that part?

## 3. The proxy chain

Trace the full chain from what is recorded to what matters:

response or observation → score → property → real-world outcome

At each arrow, ask what licenses the inference.

- Show a false positive and a false negative for this measure.
- What happens to the measure when people or systems are trained, selected, rewarded or optimised against it? (A measure that becomes a target can stop measuring.)
- Does averaging hide rare but severe cases?
- Are there ceiling or floor effects?
- Is the measure contaminated (the answers known in advance), saturated (everyone scores near the top), or exploitable through surface cues?
- What decision threshold is proposed, and who bears the cost of each kind of error?

## 4. Validity

Ask about each kind separately. A study can be strong on one and weak on another.

| Kind | Question |
|---|---|
| Construct | Does the measure capture the concept, and only the concept? |
| Content | Does it cover the full range of the concept? |
| Convergent | Does it agree with other measures of the same concept? |
| Discriminant | Does it disagree with measures of different concepts? |
| Criterion or predictive | Does it predict the outcomes the concept should predict? |
| Internal | Does the design support the causal or comparative conclusion drawn? |
| External | Would the result hold for other populations, settings, times or systems? |
| Ecological | Does the setting resemble the one the conclusion is about? |
| Statistical conclusion | Are the statistical inferences sound? (see `statistical-inference.md`) |

## 5. Reliability

- Would the measure give the same result if repeated (test-retest)?
- Would two raters, coders or graders agree (inter-rater)?
- Do the items in a scale measure the same thing (internal consistency)?
- Is the measure equally valid across the groups being compared (measurement invariance)?
- How sensitive is it to small changes in wording, format, instructions, tooling or rater?

## 6. Qualitative operationalisation

For interviews, observation, documents and other qualitative material:

- How are codes or themes defined, and how was the coding frame developed?
- Were codes applied consistently? Was this checked?
- How would a different analyst's reading differ?
- Is there a clear trail from raw material to interpretation?

## Watch for

- Construct substitution: measuring something easier that merely correlates with the target.
- A thin measure presented as capturing a rich concept.
- Validity assumed from face plausibility.
- A measure validated in one population or setting used without checking in another.
- A metric that has become a target.

## Artefacts

- **Construct map**: each dimension of the concept linked to a candidate measure, its known failure modes, a validation test, and the claims the study would not be entitled to make.
- **Validity argument**: the case for the measure under each kind of validity, with the gaps marked.
- **Proxy chain**: each link from observation to outcome with its licensing evidence and its weakest point.
