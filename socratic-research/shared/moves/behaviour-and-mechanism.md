# Behaviour and mechanism

Use this move when the work infers something internal (a process, disposition, intention, representation, strategy, belief, capacity or cause) from what can be observed from outside. The gap between observed behaviour and the inferred mechanism is one of the most common places where research overclaims, in any field that studies people, animals, organisations, cells, materials, software or machine learning systems.

## Use when

- An internal property is inferred from outputs, choices, responses or measurements.
- A behaviour is explained by appeal to what is going on inside.
- Intentional or mental language is applied to a system.
- Internal measurements (imaging, probes, logs, instrumentation) are used to support a mechanistic claim.

## 1. What kind of claim

- Is the claim that the mechanism exists, that it causes the behaviour, that it explains the behaviour, or only that it predicts it?
- Is the property defined by outputs, by internal states, by dispositions, or by what would happen under other conditions?
- At what level is it described: component, subsystem, whole system, population?

## 2. Rival mechanisms

List what else could produce the same behaviour. Common rivals across fields:

- memorisation, habit or learned association;
- simple heuristics or surface cues;
- artefacts of the task, instrument, format or instructions;
- limits of ability or capacity, rather than choice;
- external constraints, rules or incentives;
- strategic adaptation to being observed (see `reactivity-and-observer-effects.md`);
- chance or noise;
- a different internal process that happens to give the same outputs here.

For each rival: how plausible is it, and how much would it change the conclusion?

## 3. Separating them

- What intervention would give different predictions under the target mechanism and under each rival? (Manipulating a component, removing it, changing the input, changing the incentive.)
- What counterfactual or out-of-distribution test would separate them?
- What internal measurement would separate them, and is that measurement itself open to rival interpretations?
- Would convergent evidence from several independent methods narrow the field?

## 4. Inference in both directions

- Does absence of the behaviour show absence of the property? Or could the property be present but not expressed here?
- Does presence of the behaviour show possession of the property? Or could the behaviour occur without it?
- Would a more capable, motivated or adaptive subject be better at showing, hiding or simulating this behaviour?

## 5. Internal evidence

When the support comes from looking inside:

- How were the internal features selected and labelled? Was the interpretation fixed before examining behaviour?
- Does the method find a mechanism the system uses, or information that is merely present?
- How sensitive are the findings to method choices: which region, layer, time window, threshold or basis?
- Does manipulating the internal feature change the behaviour as predicted?

## 6. Language

- Is intentional or mental vocabulary ("wants", "believes", "knows", "decides", "deceives", "understands") used? What is its functional or evidential basis here?
- Can the claim be restated in terms that behavioural evidence alone supports? What is lost in the restatement?

## Watch for

- One behavioural signature treated as diagnostic when several mechanisms produce it.
- Internal evidence treated as settling a question it is itself underdetermined on.
- Mechanistic language attached to purely behavioural results.
- Failure to elicit a behaviour treated as proof the capacity is absent.

## Artefacts

- **Underdetermination map**: the observed behaviour, each candidate mechanism, what each predicts, and the tests that separate them.
- **Licensed conclusions**: separate statements of what the behavioural evidence, the internal evidence, and the convergent evidence each support.
- **Calibrated wording**: the claim rewritten at the strength the evidence licenses.
