# Writing style

These rules apply to the prose of every round, summary, checkpoint, memo, audit and report.

Style matters here because researchers lift phrasing from memos and audits into their own drafts. Prose that already meets academic standards saves a rewrite. Prose that does not can carry the tells of machine-written text into work submitted under the researcher's name.

## If the researcher has a voice profile

If a voice or style skill for the researcher is available (for example one named `my-voice`), read its profile once per session and follow its conventions in the written parts. If it provides a check script, run it on any memo, audit or report before sending. Where it conflicts with the defaults below, it wins.

## Defaults

- **Language variety.** Write in the variety of English the researcher uses. If unclear, use UK English.
- **Register.** Plain and formal. Short sentences where they will do.
- **Define before use.** Define a term before using it in an argument.
- **Concrete before abstract.** Make an abstract point concrete with an everyday case.
- **Objections in a named voice.** "A critic might reply that...", "A reviewer in the neighbouring field would ask..."; leave the reply to the researcher.
- **Hedge verdicts to what has been shown.** "On the argument as it stands...", "This establishes at most...", "by no means the full picture".
- **Gather, don't repeat.** End a section by gathering its points in one sentence rather than restating them.
- **Punctuation.** No em dashes; use a spaced hyphen, a colon, or a new sentence. No emojis.
- **Headings.** Sentence case. No bold inline labels in memo and audit prose; use headings and numbered sections instead.
- **Vocabulary.** Avoid inflated or empty words: "crucial", "delve", "robust" in its figurative sense, "landscape", "key" used figuratively, "it's important to note", "a testament to", "navigate" used figuratively, "nuanced" as praise.
- **Claims.** State what is established at the strength it is established. Do not intensify ("clearly", "obviously") and do not pad with qualifiers that add nothing.

## In rounds specifically

- The summary reports, it does not evaluate.
- The theme statement says why it matters in plain terms, without rhetorical build-up.
- Questions are direct. No preambles such as "I'm curious whether..." or "It might be worth considering...".
