# The end-of-session audit

The audit is the main deliverable of a session. It records what was examined, what held up, what changed, and what remains to be done, in enough detail that the researcher can act on it without the transcript.

This file defines the shared skeleton. Each category skill adds sections of its own and says how to fill the shared ones for its kind of research. Use the skeleton and the category's additions together.

## When

- When the researcher asks to stop.
- When the arc is complete.
- When the planned rounds are used and the researcher does not want to extend.
- As the closing part of a written memo.

Before writing it, run the Claude self-audit in `self-scrutiny.md` on your own contributions to the session.

## Origin tags

Tag every item in the audit by where it came from:

- `[supplied]`: material the researcher gave at intake;
- `[researcher]`: what the researcher said during the dialogue;
- `[Claude]`: Claude's reconstruction, inference or suggestion;
- `[open]`: unresolved.

The tags matter because the researcher may reuse the audit in their own work. Claude's suggestions must never read as the researcher's conclusions, and an unresolved point must never read as settled.

## Rules

- Write each section in full sentences, with enough detail to act on.
- Use the numbered section headings; avoid bold inline labels.
- Write "None found" rather than inventing content for an empty section.
- If the inquiry did not justify a determinate conclusion, say exactly what remains underdetermined and what would settle it. Aporia located precisely is a result; smoothing it into a tidy answer throws that result away.
- Follow `writing-style.md` and `evidence-integrity.md` throughout.

## Shared sections

Every audit contains these sections, in this order. Category sections are inserted where each category skill specifies.

1. **Initial position, verbatim.** The central claim and its supporting structure as the researcher first stated them.
2. **Claim inventory.** The table from `claim-sorting.md`, with kinds, strength, scope, load-bearing marks and bridges.
3. **Clarified terms.** For each load-bearing term: the meaning settled in the session, what it excludes, and any remaining indeterminacy.
4. **Reconstructed structure.** The confirmed reconstruction (argument, model, design, specification, or study), and the steelman.
5. **Load-bearing assumptions, ranked.** Each with how much depends on it, how contestable it is, and its tier: dealbreaker (if false, the work produces nothing), important (if false, the work changes but survives), secondary (affects extensions only).
6. **Strongest support.** The best reasons, evidence or results for the position, and what each actually establishes.
7. **Strongest challenge and the best reply.** The most serious objection, counterexample, rival account or alternative explanation found, and the best reply the session produced.
8. **Inconsistent sets and repairs.** Each smallest inconsistent set found, and the repair chosen, or that it remains open.
9. **Problems found, classified.** Every problem, with its class from `contradictions.md` and the specific premise, step or element it attaches to.
10. **Revision log.** What changed, at which round, and why.
11. **Strongest surviving position.** The best version of the position after the session, in the researcher's terms where possible.
12. **What the work does not license.** Conclusions the current argument or evidence does not support, stated plainly, including any the researcher may be tempted to draw.
13. **Remaining uncertainty.** What is still underdetermined, and what would settle it.
14. **Needs sources, evidence or argument.** Every claim marked `[SOURCE NEEDED]`, `[EVIDENCE NEEDED]` or needing an argument, with what would satisfy it.
15. **Research tasks.** Every open question converted into a task with a completion criterion: "Done when ...". Order them by priority, with the cheapest check of the riskiest dealbreaker assumption first.
16. **Confidence update.** How confidence in the position should change after the session, and why. Separate confidence in the claim from confidence in the current support for it.
17. **Recommendation.** Continue, revise, narrow, or stop, with reasons.

## Fitting the audit to the depth

- **Light** sessions: sections 1, 4, 5, 7, 9, 11, 12, 15 and 17 are required; include the others where the session produced content for them.
- **Standard** and **deep** sessions: every section.

## Opening for written memos

When the audit closes a written memo, open the memo with the three most consequential questions, so a reader with ten minutes gets the core (see `report-variants.md`).
