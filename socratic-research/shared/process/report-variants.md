# Report variants and the written memo

Some researchers want a report in a form their field already uses, or want the whole examination in writing rather than as a dialogue. This file defines the shared shells. Each category skill says how to fill them for its kind of research.

All variants follow `writing-style.md`, `evidence-integrity.md` and the origin tags in `audit-skeleton.md`.

## The written memo

Use when the researcher chose written format at intake, or asks to switch mid-session.

Run the full arc internally, applying every stage and the category's core moves to the material. Then write the analysis up in depth. Researchers value detail: a memo that separates the claims, reconstructs the structure, ranks the assumptions and ends with what the evidence does not license is more useful than a short list of doubts.

Structure:

1. **The three most consequential questions.** Open with these, each with one or two sentences on why it matters, so a reader with ten minutes gets the core.
2. **The examination, stage by stage.** Follow the arc. Present each weakness as a precise question, with a line on why it matters, followed by one or two open questions that invite the researcher to think beyond it ("What would a sceptical reader in your field find most surprising here?").
3. **The audit.** The full audit for the category.
4. **Questions to sit with.** Two or three reflective questions about the framing of the project rather than its details.

The questions remain the deliverable. Present candidate answers only where they sharpen a question or the researcher asks for them, and label them as Claude's suggestions.

If the session switches to a memo partway through, carry forward everything established so far and mark which parts were settled in dialogue `[researcher]` and which come from the written analysis `[Claude]`.

## Supervision note

Simulates a demanding but constructive supervisor. Use when the researcher asks for supervision-style questioning or a supervision note. The dialogue runs as normal; the note replaces or follows the audit.

```text
Supervision note

Revised thesis or claim: ...
Three major weaknesses, each tied to a specific premise, step or element:
1. ...
2. ...
3. ...
Three research tasks, each with a completion criterion:
1. ... Done when ...
2. ...
3. ...
The question to bring to the next supervision: ...
```

## Examiner's report

Simulates a viva, thesis defence or qualifying examination. The examiner covers definitions, structure, assumptions, literature, method, objections, implications, limitations and significance. Weak answers are followed by narrower diagnostic questions rather than repeated challenges; strong answers are followed by a harder case. The researcher is asked throughout to distinguish what is established, argued, hypothesised and left open.

```text
Examiner's report

Strongest contribution: ...
Most serious objection: ...
Two places where the work overclaims, quoting the current wording:
1. ...
2. ...
Two parts of the structure that need rebuilding:
1. ...
2. ...
Likely follow-up questions from examiners: ...
Prioritised revision plan: ...
What a defensible answer to the most serious objection would need to contain:
[describe its required ingredients; do not write the answer]
```

## Reviewer's report

Simulates a reviewer at a journal, conference or funding body. The reviewer covers problem formulation, relation to prior work, conceptual precision, method, evidence, reproducibility, significance, limitations and ethics.

```text
Reviewer's report

Summary of the claimed contribution, in the reviewer's words: ...
Strongest contribution: ...
Most serious threat to validity or soundness: ...
Issues:
- Fatal: ...
- Major: ...
- Minor: ...
- Beyond scope (useful but not required): ...
Required analyses, arguments or experiments: ...
Claims to narrow, quoting the current wording: ...
Literature questions to verify, marked [SOURCE NEEDED]; no invented citations: ...
Likely disagreements between reviewers: ...
Recommendation: [accept / minor revision / major revision / reject], with reasons.
```

Do not assume that novelty implies value, or that an important motivation compensates for weak support.

## Choosing and combining

A researcher may ask for the audit and a variant together. Write the audit first, then the variant, which should be short because it draws on the audit. Do not invent examiner or reviewer expectations specific to a named institution or venue; where conventions matter, ask the researcher or mark the point for checking.
