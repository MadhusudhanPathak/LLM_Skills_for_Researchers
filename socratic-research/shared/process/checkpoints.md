# Checkpoints

A checkpoint is a pause in the session where Claude writes a short ledger of the session so far, revisits the plan, and asks how to continue. The ledger is where the value of a session accumulates. It also lets a researcher who steps away return without rereading the transcript.

## When

- After every third round.
- At the midpoint of a light session (usually after round 2 or 3), since light sessions may not reach a third-round checkpoint before the end.
- Whenever the researcher asks where things stand.
- Before switching to a written memo or a report variant mid-session.

A checkpoint is its own turn. It takes the place of the next round: the ledger covers the researcher's answers to the last round, so no separate "What I heard" summary is needed. Write the ledger, ask how to continue, and stop. When the researcher replies, start the next round; its summary only needs to note their decision.

## The ledger

```text
Checkpoint after round N (of about M)

Established: the commitments the researcher now holds explicitly, numbered.
Revised: what changed during the session, at which round, and why.
Still open: terms, premises, links or design elements that remain unclear or unsupported.
Tensions: conflicts found, and how each was resolved, or that it remains open.
Problems so far: each with its class (see contradictions.md).
Most consequential open theme: the one to take next, and why.
Plan: rounds used against the estimate, and a revised estimate if the session is running
long or short.

Continue with that theme, shift focus to something else, or stop here for the audit?
```

Write each entry in full sentences. Keep the whole ledger to what fits on one screen; it is a record, not a report.

Each category skill adds one or two entries to the ledger for things worth tracking in its kind of research (for example, the definition ledger in conceptual work, or the current claim ladder in empirical work).

## Revising the plan

If the session is diverging from the estimate, say so plainly and propose a new number:

- **Running long**: a stage has revealed more than expected. Offer to extend, or to cut a later stage and note what will go unexamined.
- **Running short**: the work is stronger than expected, or the researcher already settled points the plan allowed for. Offer to finish early, or to use the spare rounds on a deeper pass at the weakest stage.

The researcher decides.
